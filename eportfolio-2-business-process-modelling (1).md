### Summary

This article explains how BPMN was used to model the processes of a printing company. The researchers looked at ordering, buying materials, printing, collecting goods and delivery. ## Artefact 2 — Research Article: Model-Based Systems Engineering and BPM

**Source:** Sullivan, Arias-Nava and Patel (2025), *Model-Based Systems Engineering for Business Process Management: Leveraging ARCADIA for Production System Design*.

[View Artefact 2](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/18479790251387385)

### Summary

This article explains how business process models can be connected with technical system models. The authors use the ARCADIA method and Capella software to show workers, tasks, business needs and system parts. Their manufacturing example shows that models can make complex processes easier to understand. They can also improve communication and help organisations notice problems earlier (Sullivan, Arias-Nava & Patel 2025).

### Justification and Reflection

I chose this article because static and dynamic models are discussed in Week 5, Slide 6, while business process models are discussed in Slide 16. It helped me understand that a model can show business activities and connect them with the technical system. This is useful because business and technical workers need to understand the same process. This artefact shows my BPM knowledge because a model should be suitable for its purpose and its users.

## Artefact 3 — Research Paper: BPMN Assistant

**Source:** Licardo, Tanković and Etinger (2026), *BPMN Assistant: An LLM-Based Approach to Business Process Modeling*.

[View Artefact 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24592)

### Summary

This paper presents BPMN Assistant, which uses AI to create and edit BPMN diagrams from written instructions. The tool first puts the process information into a simpler structure and then creates the BPMN file. The testing showed that this method was more reliable than creating the technical file directly. It was also faster and required less output (Licardo, Tanković & Etinger 2026, pp. 1–3, 16–17).

### Justification and Reflection

I chose this paper because BPMN is discussed in Week 4, Slide 23, and modelling tools are discussed in Week 5, Slide 22. It helped me understand how AI could make process modelling easier for people who do not have much technical knowledge. However, a person still needs to check the final diagram because AI can make mistakes or misunderstand instructions. This artefact shows my BPM knowledge because a useful model must be clear and correct, no matter which tool creates it.

## Artefact 4 — Review Article: Value Stream Mapping and Process Mining

**Source:** Teriete, Erlach, Bauernhansl and van der Aalst (2026), *Combining Value Stream Mapping and Process Mining in Production: A Systematic Literature Review*.

[View Artefact 4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-026-08446-y)

### Summary

This article reviews 21 studies that combine value stream mapping with process mining. Value stream mapping shows the full process and helps organisations find waste, such as waiting and unnecessary work. Process mining uses recorded data to show what actually happens. The authors explain that using both methods can provide a clear overview and more detailed information, but better tools are still needed (Teriete et al. 2026).

### Justification and Reflection

I chose this article because value stream mapping is discussed in Week 4, Slide 26. It helped me understand that a manual map may only show the process at one point in time. Process mining can add recorded information and show how the process changes. This artefact shows my BPM knowledge because organisations should choose a modelling method based on the information and amount of detail they need.
